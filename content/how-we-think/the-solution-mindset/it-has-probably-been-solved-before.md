> Before you decide a task is hard, ask one question: has this been done before, and how many times? For most of what you will ever be asked to do, the answer is thousands of times, by people no smarter than you, with the solution sitting in the open where anyone can read it. That changes the task completely. Instead of inventing an answer, you are finding one that already exists and fitting it to your case.

## Almost nothing you do is new

The feeling that a task is difficult often comes from treating it as if you are the first person ever to face it. You are almost never the first. Building a login screen, filing taxes in a new country, wiring a light fixture, setting up a small online store: each of these has been done so many times that the path is well marked. The difficulty you feel is the difficulty of not yet knowing the path, and that is a different, much smaller problem than the difficulty of the thing itself.

## When the answer already exists, the work is finding it

Think about what it would take to prove a famous, genuinely hard piece of mathematics from scratch. For almost anyone, impossible. Now think about what it takes to find that same proof today. You search for it. It is written down, explained, and sitting online, and even a chatbot can walk you through it. The proof did not get easier. The hardest part, coming up with it, was done once by someone else and then published for everyone.

Most tasks are like this. The expensive part, working out how, has already been paid for by the thousands of people who did it before you. What is left for you is to find their answer and apply it to your case. That is real work, but it is the work of looking, reading, and adapting, and it is well within reach.

## "Hard" is often just "unfamiliar"

Watch how often "hard" turns out to mean "I have not looked yet."

- Someone treats packaging up an app to share as a daunting technical feat. That exact thing happens hundreds of thousands of times a day, and the steps are written down in a dozen places.
- Someone calls a standard product feature too complex to attempt, when ten thousand versions of it are deployed and running right now, most of them findable and readable.

If a thing exists and works in ten thousand places, there is no special reason it cannot work in yours. Nothing about the task blocks you except that you have not yet seen how it is already done.

## Ten minutes of looking

When something feels too hard to start, spend the first ten minutes searching instead of worrying. Look for how the problem has already been solved: a guide, an open example, a working project you can read. Most of the time you will find that the wall you were staring at has a door in it that thousands of people already walked through. Then your job is the manageable one of following them.
