> If people have to chase you for a reply, or double-check everything you send, then being around you is extra work. Two habits fix most of it: answer before you are chased, and check what you send before it leaves your hands. Both come down to not making your reliability someone else's job.

## If they have to chase you, that's on you

Here is the healthy way to read a reminder: if someone had to chase you, something slipped, and it is yours to tighten up. Here is the version to avoid: treating it as normal that people ask two or three times before they hear back from you.

Whoever is waiting on you should not have to work to get your attention. When a client or a teammate has to send the third "any update?", treat it as a sign that answering on time had quietly stopped happening, and fix that. The person waiting is not the one who needs to change. Being easy to reach, and quick to close the loop, is a large part of what makes people trust working with you.

## Check what you send when you speak for others

The second habit is about what leaves your hands, especially when you represent more than yourself. A message to a client, an email under the company's name, anything public: check it before it goes. Is it clear? Is it correct? Does it actually say what you mean?

Be honest with yourself about where you need a second look. If you are not fully sure your wording or your facts are right, verify them, with a tool, a quick check, or another person. There is no shame in checking, and plenty in sending something wrong under a name that belongs to more people than you. Representing a group raises the bar on everything you put out, and the fix is cheap: look before you send.

## Make your reliability your own job

Aim to be the person nobody has to manage. Reply before the second reminder arrives. Read back what you are about to send as a stranger would, and fix what is unclear or wrong before it goes. The goal is simple: people should be able to hand you something, or wait on you for something, and then stop thinking about it, because they know it is handled.
