> Managing is a role, and it lands on far more people than the ones with "manager" in their title. The moment you speak for a group, or others depend on what you do, you are managing something. It is worth knowing what that asks of you, whatever your actual job is.

## The role behind the title

A manager is anyone holding a certain kind of responsibility, whatever their title says. And that responsibility finds people who never asked for it.

Two everyday cases. The first: you get on a call with a client, alone. For that call, you are the whole company in their eyes. You are managing the relationship, whether or not you manage any people. The second: other work depends on your piece being ready. You are now managing a thing others rely on, again with no title involved. In both, the role showed up on its own.

## What the role asks of you

When you hold it, three things change. You carry responsibility for more than your own task. You speak and act on behalf of other people, so your words are read as theirs. And people judge the whole group through you, because in that moment you are what they can see.

None of that requires a promotion. It requires noticing when you have stepped into the role, and then rising to it.

## Spot the moment you step into it

Watch for the moments the role lands on you. A conversation where you represent more than yourself. A task others are waiting on. A message that goes out under the group's name. When you notice one, treat it the way a manager would: as something you are responsible for, rather than something happening around you. The title is optional. The responsibility is already yours.
