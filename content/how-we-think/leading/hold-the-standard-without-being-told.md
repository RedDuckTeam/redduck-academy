> Here is a tension people rarely notice in themselves. They dislike being told exactly how to do their work, and they also want every expectation written down for them in advance. Both wishes cannot hold at once. The way through is to carry the obvious standards yourself, without waiting to be asked.

## The contradiction

The same person who bristles at being micromanaged will often say, when something is wrong, "but nobody told me it had to work on a phone," or "handle a bad input," or "not fall over when one small thing fails."

Those are part of what doing the job well means, too obvious for anyone to write down. A website that only works on the tester's own screen is not finished. Software that crashes on the first odd input is not done. If a standard that basic had to be spelled out for you, then the judgment you were brought in for is missing. You cannot both refuse close direction and demand that everything be directed.

## The perfect instruction is the finished work

Think about what a truly complete set of instructions would look like. It would specify every behavior, every edge, every response to every situation. At that point the instructions are the finished work itself. There is nothing left to build.

Since that complete specification never exists in advance, part of every real job is filling the gap with judgment, the standards everyone competent in your field already carries. That gap is exactly where skill lives. A cleaner does not need "also, do not break the vase" written into the instructions. Not breaking the vase is part of cleaning well.

## The same holds outside work

You do not need a friend to tell you, in advance, not to read their private messages while you are borrowing their phone. Basic decency is a standard you hold on your own, and no one has to hand it to you as a rule. Most of what makes someone good to work with, or good to know, is standards they keep without being told, in the space no instruction covers.

## Bring the standard with you

When you take on anything, assume the obvious standards apply even though no one listed them. Ask what "done well" means to someone who knows the work, and hold yourself to that standard as much as to the words you were given. The people who are trusted with more are the ones who bring the standard with them, instead of waiting for it to be written down.
