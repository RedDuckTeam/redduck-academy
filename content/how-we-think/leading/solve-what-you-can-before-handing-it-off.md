> Before you pass a problem to someone else, find out how much of it you can handle yourself. A surprising amount of what gets labeled "not my area" turns out to be ordinary work you could do with a little effort. Handing it off is the last step, after trying, and it is a lot rarer than it feels.

## Much of "not my area" is ordinary work

A task comes up that sounds specialized, so the instinct is to send it straight to a specialist. Often that instinct is wrong. Plenty of things that sound like expert work are done by ordinary people every day.

Take moving money or assets from one account to another. To someone who has never done it, it can sound technical and risky, a job for an expert. But millions of people who know nothing about the machinery underneath do exactly that, every day, from their phones. Something sounding like a specialist's job does not make it one. Check whether it truly needs an expert before you decide it does.

## Know the fundamentals of your own work

There is a related habit worth guarding against: handing off every real part of your own job and keeping only the label. Someone who calls themselves the "ideas" person, while unable to do or even explain the actual work, has not found a clever arrangement. Knowing less about your craft does not make you more valuable at it.

Understand the fundamentals of what you do, enough to reason about it, catch obvious mistakes, and not go helpless the moment a real question appears. You can leave the deepest expertise to specialists. The basics of your own craft are yours to hold, and a title or a role is no substitute for holding them.

## The tools have moved the bar

It is easier than ever to handle something yourself before passing it on. Between search and modern assistants, you can usually learn enough to do an unfamiliar task in minutes, or at least enough to try it sensibly. The honest threshold for "I genuinely cannot do this" is higher than it used to be, because so much is now a few questions away.

## Try it before you pass it

When something lands on you that feels outside your area, spend a little time seeing how far you can get before handing it on. Look up how it is done. Attempt the first step. Often you will finish it yourself, and even when you cannot, you will hand over a much clearer problem. Passing work along is sometimes right, but only after an honest try, never in place of one.
