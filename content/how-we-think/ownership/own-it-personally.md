> When a task belongs to "the team," or to "we," it often belongs to no one. Responsibility spread evenly across a group has a way of evaporating. The fix is simple, and a little uncomfortable: every task has one specific person who owns it, by name.

## Why "we'll handle it" quietly fails

There is a name for what goes wrong here: collective irresponsibility. When a job is everyone's, each person quietly assumes someone else has it, and so nobody does. No one decided to drop it. It fell through the space between them.

Picture a group that agrees, together, to send a client an important link by the end of the day. Everyone heard it. Everyone agreed. The day ends and the link was never sent, and stranger still, nobody said a word about it. Not because the people were lazy. Because "we" owned the task, and "we" is not a person who can be counted on to act.

## One name on every task

The cure is to give each task a single owner: one person, chosen and named. That person may not do all the work, but they are the one who makes sure it happens, and the one you ask about it.

When a task seems to have two owners and it is unclear who acts, treat that as a problem to fix right now, before the task itself. Split it into parts and give each part one owner. Ambiguity about who is responsible is exactly the condition where things fall through, so it is the first thing to remove.

## It works the same at home

This is not only a work idea. A shared house where "someone" should deal with the bins is a house with overflowing bins. A group trip where "we" should book the place is a trip with nowhere to stay. The moment one person says "I've got the booking," it gets done.

## Put your name on it

When you are in a group and something needs doing, be the one who says "I'll own this," and say it out loud so everyone knows. And when you are handing work out, resist leaving it with "the team." Ask who, specifically, is on the hook. A task with a name attached gets done. A task owned by everyone waits for no one in particular.
