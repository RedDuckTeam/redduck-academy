> A problem you keep to yourself only grows. The moment you hit one, say so. Most problems have a cheap fix while they are small and fresh, and an expensive one once they have sat in silence for a week.

## A problem hidden is a problem growing

Someone is asked to study a recording and write up what is in it. The recording will not play for them. Instead of saying so, they work around it, guessing from other sources, and produce a write-up about the wrong thing. Days later, someone checks, and by then the original recording has been deleted. The one chance to get it right is gone.

All of that came from a single unspoken problem. "The recording will not play" is a five-second message. Sent the moment it happened, it costs nothing and the recording is still there to fix. Held back, it quietly destroyed the whole task.

## Raising it early is the strong move

Some people sit on problems because raising one feels like admitting they could not cope. It does the opposite. A problem raised early, while it is small, is easy to help with, and it makes you look like someone on top of their work. The same problem found late, after your silence let it grow, is the thing that actually looks bad.

Asking for help the moment you are stuck is a strong move. Often the fix is one sentence from someone who has seen it before, and you only get that sentence if you speak up while there is still time to use it.

## Say it while it's small

Treat the moment you notice a problem as the moment to name it, before you have tried to quietly paper over it. A short, early "here is what is blocking me" gives everyone the best and cheapest chance to solve it. Waiting rarely makes a problem smaller. It just adds your silence to the list of things that went wrong.
