> When you are waiting on someone else, that time does not have to be dead. Hand off what they need, then keep moving on something else. Done right, their part finishes in the background while you work, and the wait costs you almost nothing.

## The cost of standing in line

Someone needs funds sent to an account before they can test something. So they ask for the account details. But instead of sharing the details and carrying on with other work, they finish everything else first, then share the details, and then sit idle, waiting for the transfer to arrive. The transfer they could have started an hour earlier.

Had they sent the details first, the money would have been moving while they did the rest of their work. By the time they finished, it would already be there. Same tasks, same people, but one ordering wastes an hour and the other wastes nothing.

## Start what you can, right now

The same idea shows up whenever you think you are blocked. Say you are waiting for access to a shared workspace before you can begin. You could almost always start locally, on your own machine, right now, and connect it up once access arrives. Being fully blocked is rare. Usually you are blocked on one thing and completely free to make progress on several others.

So when you notice yourself waiting, ask two questions. What can I hand off now so it runs in the background? And what can I get on with in the meantime? There is nearly always an answer to at least one of them.

## Kick it off, then keep moving

Treat every wait as something to set running and then step away from, rather than something to stand next to until it finishes. Send the thing that unblocks someone else first, before your own next task, so their part and yours run at the same time. Ordered that way, a day holds far more than it does when you do everything in a single line, one item after another.
