> Some words sound harmless and quietly hand your responsibility away. "Maybe." "Probably." "I think." "I'll try." Said out of habit, they train you to expect less of yourself. Cutting them changes more than how you sound. It changes how you act.

## "I'll try" when there is nothing to try

Someone is asked to do a task that has been done a thousand times before, something entirely routine. They answer, "I'll try to get it done." Try? The word smuggles in doubt about whether the thing is even possible, when there is no real doubt at all. It quietly lowers the bar before anyone has started.

Compare "I'll try to get it done" with a plain "I'll get it done." The first quietly leaves a soft exit for later. The second closes that exit and puts you on the hook. When a task is genuinely doable, "try" is a hedge with nothing behind it, and it is worth catching yourself using.

## The words leak into your thinking

This is bigger than how you sound to other people. Say "maybe," "I think," and "probably" often enough and you start to believe them. The habit of hedging becomes a habit of holding back, of treating outcomes as out of your hands. Language runs in both directions. It reports how you think, and it also shapes how you think. Talk as though things are beyond your control and you will slowly act that way too.

## When the hedge is honest

None of this means faking certainty you do not have. Real uncertainty deserves to be said, clearly and specifically. "I am not sure this approach will cut the load enough, and here is why" is honest and useful. That is a completely different thing from a reflexive "maybe" sprinkled over matters you are actually sure about. Keep the honest, specific doubt. Drop the automatic hedge.

## Watch the hedges

For a few days, just notice your own "maybe," "probably," and "I'll try." Each time one appears, ask whether there is real uncertainty behind it. If there is, state it plainly and specifically. If there is not, cut the word and say what you will actually do. You will sound more reliable, and over time you will feel more in control of what you take on.
