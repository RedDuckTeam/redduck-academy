> You can always make something better. There is no piece of work that more time could not improve. That is exactly why "make it perfect" is not a real goal. Perfect has no edge to stop at. The useful skill is knowing what is worth improving and what is already good enough, and then stopping.

## Where perfectionism goes wrong

It is easy to mistake perfectionism for caring about quality. They are not the same thing. A perfectionist tries to make everything as good as possible, everywhere, all the time. Someone with real judgment picks the few places where quality matters and puts their effort there.

Think about what an engineer actually does. They weigh trade-offs. More speed against more cost. More features against more time. They do not try to push every dial to its limit, because pushing one dial all the way starves the others. Trying to make everything ideal is a sign of not yet thinking like an engineer.

## Everything can be improved, so choose

Real work has limits. A budget, a deadline, a fixed number of hours. Inside those limits, your job is to spend effort where it brings real value and to leave the rest alone. The target is a clear, reachable thing: the work is solid, and it will keep working as it grows.

That is a high bar already. It rules out anything broken, fragile, or badly built. It just does not ask you to polish a corner nobody will ever touch.

## An hour spent on a tiny thing

Here is how the trap looks in real life. Five people spent fifteen minutes in a meeting arguing about one repeated line of layout, three copies of the same small thing, in a part of the screen almost nobody looks at. Five people, fifteen minutes. That is over an hour of shared time on something one person could have quietly fixed in the time it took to describe it.

The repeated line was real, and it was small. Noticing it was fine. The mistake was treating a small thing as if it were a big one, and spending an hour of the team's time proving how important it was.

## Spend your care where it counts

Before you pour effort into making something better, ask one question: will this change anything that matters? If yes, do it well. If it is a corner nobody will feel, let it be good enough and move on. Caring about everything equally is the same as not knowing what to care about.
