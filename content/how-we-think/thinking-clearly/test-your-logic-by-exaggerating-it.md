> Here is a fast way to test an argument you are unsure about. Take it and make it bigger. Push the logic to an extreme and see what it turns into. If the extreme version is clearly absurd, the smaller version was absurd too. You just could not see it at normal size.

## Turn the logic up until it breaks

Someone is handed a task that involves reading a log file on a server. They say they cannot do it, they need a server specialist, because servers are that specialist's area.

Push that logic bigger. Reading is involved, so surely they would need a reading specialist. Typing the fix means calling in a typist. Turning the computer on is really the job of a computer specialist, and getting online needs an internet specialist. Said out loud, it is plainly ridiculous. Nobody needs a typist to type.

That absurd version is the same argument as the original, only louder. "This task touches someone else's area, so I cannot do it" sounded reasonable at a whisper. Turned up, it falls apart. The task did touch a specialist's area, but touching an area is a long way from being unable to start.

## The same trick on "it is worth it"

The move works on your own reasoning too, the same way it works on other people's.

Cutting a corner often looks worth it in the moment. Save an hour, tell a small lie about how long something took, nobody notices. Now push it bigger. If quiet cheating really were the smart play, the smartest move of all would be to go rob a bank. Easy money, right there.

Obviously not. Everyone knows the bank robber is not a genius, because the costs are real and simply arrive later. The small corner-cut has the exact same shape. Its costs are real and delayed, only smaller and slower, which is exactly why they are easy to talk yourself into. Blown up to bank size the logic is clearly broken, so it was broken at small size too.

## Push it and see

When an argument feels off but you cannot say why, or when your own excuse feels a little too convenient, make it bigger. Ask what it would look like taken to the extreme. If the giant version is something no reasonable person would accept, you have your answer, and you can throw the small version out with it.
