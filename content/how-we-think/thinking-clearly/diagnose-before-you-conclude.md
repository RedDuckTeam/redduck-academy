> When something does not work, the first explanation that comes to mind is usually the weakest one. "It is broken." "It just does not work." A little real thinking about what actually happened almost always beats that first guess. Finding the true cause is a skill, and most of the skill is being willing to look.

## A small mystery

Ana writes a document and shares it with Ben so he can read it. Ben clicks the link and cannot open it. He tells Ana he has no access. Ana checks, and she is sure she gave it to him. Both of them are stuck. The easy conclusion is sitting right there: the site is broken, nothing we can do.

## The cause was in plain sight

It was not broken. When Ana opened the sharing settings and actually looked, Ben was not on the list of people with access. She had typed his address into the share box but never pressed the final confirm, so the invitation was never sent. From her side, the document looked shared. From Ben's side there was simply no access, and the page was telling him the plain truth.

Nobody needed a support ticket. One person looking closely at the real evidence, the sharing settings on Ana's screen and the exact words on Ben's, would have found the cause in under a minute. The answer was in plain sight the whole time. It just needed someone to look instead of concluding.

## Look at what actually happened

The move is always the same. When something fails, hold off on explaining it and go gather what actually happened first. The exact link. The exact error message. The exact steps that led there. Real causes are specific, and specific things can be found. Vague ones, like "it is broken", cannot be fixed, because they do not point anywhere.

None of this takes special cleverness. It takes thirty more seconds of looking before you decide there is nothing to find.

## Before you blame the tool

Next time your instinct says "this thing is just broken," treat that as a reason to slow down rather than give up. Ask what exactly happened, and go look at the real evidence. Most of the time the cause is ordinary, specific, and sitting in plain view, waiting for the first person willing to look at it directly.
