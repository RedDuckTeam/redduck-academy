> Two things turn a vague intention into a real commitment: what you will do, and by when. Drop the "by when" and you have not committed to much at all. Once you have given both, the promise is yours, to keep or to renegotiate early, and never to let quietly lapse.

## A task without a time is a wish

"I'll get to it" is not a commitment. It has no edge, so nobody can plan around it and you cannot be held to it. Attaching a time changes that. "I'll have it to you by Thursday" is something real, for you and for everyone waiting on it.

What if you honestly do not know how long it will take? Then that is the first task: give a time by which you will have an estimate. "I need to look into this. I'll tell you by tomorrow midday how long it will take." Now even your uncertainty has a shape, and nobody is left waiting on nothing.

## A promise is yours to keep

Once you say you will do something by a certain time, that agreement belongs to you. It does not matter whether it was written down, said on a call, or agreed in passing. If you gave your word, keeping track of it is your job, and no one else's.

There is one honest way out of a promise, and that is to renegotiate it early. If you can see you will not make it, say so before the deadline and give a new one. That is a completely different thing from the deadline simply passing while you say nothing.

## Silence is the real failure

Picture someone who says a piece of work will be ready by the end of the day. The day ends. The work is not ready, and there is no message, no heads-up, nothing. The next morning it is still missing, and still no word.

The missed deadline is a small problem. Things run late. The silence is the real problem. One short message the evening before, "this is going to slip to tomorrow, here is why," would have cost nothing and kept the trust intact. Holding it back is what does the damage.

## Name the date, then own it

Whenever you take something on, say when it will be done, out loud and specific. Then treat that date as yours to protect. If it is going to move, you are the one who says so, early, with a new date attached. Handled that way, being late now and then costs you almost nothing, because people trust someone who keeps them informed.
