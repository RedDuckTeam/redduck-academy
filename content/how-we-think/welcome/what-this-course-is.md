This is a course about how you think and act, at work and everywhere else. It teaches a way of approaching problems, other people, and your own commitments, drawn from the principles RedDuck runs on. Nothing to install, no syntax to memorize, just the thinking underneath. The lessons are short. Each one holds a single idea you can start using the same day.

## What you'll get from it

Most of what feels like a hard problem is really a way of looking at it. Two people meet the same task. One spends the afternoon explaining why it is hard and who else they need. The other has already finished it. The difference is rarely knowledge. It is how each of them decided to think about the task before touching it. This course is about that decision, made on purpose, over and over, until it runs on its own.

The ideas fall into a few groups. How to point yourself at a solution. How to keep your thinking clear when a problem is noisy. How to communicate so the other person actually understands you. How to own what you take on. How to act when you are the one others depend on. None of it is specific to writing code. The same habits decide how well you handle a landlord, a group trip, or a hard conversation with someone close to you.

## How to read it

Read slowly. These lessons are short on purpose, and the point is to catch yourself using them. Finishing the list matters far less than that. A lesson has done its job the first time you notice yourself about to narrate a problem and choose to solve it instead, or about to send a one-word "done" and choose to write the full picture.
