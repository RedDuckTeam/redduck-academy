> The most useful thing you can say is often the one you are tempted to keep to yourself. A worry, a better idea, a disagreement with the whole direction. Saying it is a service, even when it is uncomfortable, and swallowing it to keep the peace usually costs more than it saves.

## The unspoken concern comes back

People stay quiet to avoid friction. It feels considerate, and it feels safe. But the concern you did not raise does not disappear. It waits, and then it arrives later as a problem nobody was warned about, at a point where it is far more expensive to deal with.

An unsaid fact can quietly wreck a task, and an unsaid judgment does the same. If you can see that something is heading the wrong way, your seeing it does no good while it stays in your head. The value only exists once you say it out loud.

## Even the big disagreements

This holds for the small notes and the large ones alike. You can say that a plan looks wrong. You can say that something has been built so poorly it would be better to start over. You can say that the whole idea, the one everyone seems excited about, might be a mistake.

Delivered as honest, useful information, all of that is welcome on a good team. A group that only wants to hear agreement is weaker for it. The alternative view is worth hearing even when it turns out to be wrong, because the one time it is right can save everyone.

## How to say it well

Speaking up is not the same as complaining, and the difference is in the delivery. Say it early, while there is still time to act. Be specific about what you see. Offer the alternative if you have one. And aim the words at the problem rather than at a person, so they are easy to take in. The goal is to hand over information the group can use, so make it as usable as you can.

## Say it anyway

When you feel the small pull to stay quiet, to not make waves, to let someone else raise it, treat that pull as a signal. It usually means the thing is worth saying. Say it, early and plainly, and let the group decide what to do with it. The worst outcome is rarely that you spoke. It is far more often that nobody did.
